## The problem

The Helpdesk mobile app lets agents and admins handle conversations away from a desk. The bar was simple: it shouldn't feel like a lesser version of the web app. The home screen didn't meet it.

I started with a heuristic audit of the live Android app, screen by screen, and split what I found into two groups.

### Visual issues

- The status bar was **solid primary green**, pulling attention away from the actual content.
- The top navigation had **uneven spacing**, and unread counts sat in brackets, like "(12)".
- The message list was **text-heavy**, with no breathing room between conversations.
- The bottom navigation was about **56px tall with no labels**, and its icons used different line weights. The profile icon didn't match the others.
- The notification icon **kept blinking**, a constant distraction.

### Usability issues

- **Search and Filter**, two of the most-used actions, especially for admins, sat **at the top of the screen, out of thumb reach**.
- **Notifications** had the prime slot next to Chats in the bottom navigation, even though in our app they don't lead to any task.
- The layout didn't leave room for **Android's gesture navigation**.

## My role

I ran the audit and did the redesign as the product designer on the mobile app.

## What the audit called for

### 1. Arrange the screen by how often things are used

Bring search and filter **down into the thumb zone**, and take Notifications out of the second slot in the bottom navigation, so that position goes to something agents actually use.

### 2. Calm the screen down

A neutral status bar, consistent spacing, plain number badges instead of brackets, and more space between conversations. No blinking. The conversations should be the loudest thing on screen.

### 3. Fix the bottom navigation properly

A taller bar, a text label under every icon, one icon line weight throughout, and room for Android's gesture area.

## Outcome

The redesigned home screen shipped. I then ran the same audit-first approach on the app's contacts, notifications and profile screens.

## What I'd do differently

This was a heuristic audit, not research. I spotted the thumb-zone problem by looking at the screen, not by watching an agent use it on a busy shift. A few sessions with agents on their own phones would have confirmed the priorities and might have shown problems that a screenshot can't.
